# Scene 1: The railway house, before work
[Narrator] In the winter of nineteen thirty-six, we lived in a railway house in Mukden, in Manchukuo. The bedroom windows flowered with ice on the inside.
[Narrator] Aiko would breathe on a pane and rub a little opening with her sleeve. Through it we could see the coal merchant's horse, standing with one hind leg folded, patient as an old employee.
[Narrator] I was thirty-eight, an assistant section chief in the freight accounts department of the South Manchuria Railway. My father had sold lamp oil in a town where the rain came through our shop roof.
[Narrator] Here we had a salary, a company doctor, and enough coal. I wanted my father to have lived long enough to visit.
[Aiko] お弁当、今日は忘れないでね。
[Narrator] Aiko reminded me to take my lunch. The previous day I had carried an empty document case to the office and left my food beside the door.
[Narrator] She found this funnier than it deserved. When she laughed, the little gap between her front teeth appeared, and I still felt the surprise of having married her.
[pause 1.5]
[Narrator] Before I left, she shook coal dust from my scarf. It settled on the newspaper beneath our shoes, across a speech about the happiness of the new state.

# Scene 2: The walk to the accounts office, morning
[pause 2.5]
[Narrator] At half past eight I walked through the railway quarter to the office. Beyond its broad roads, the older city lay under a low brown sky.
[Narrator] A man sold hot sweet potatoes from a drum. I bought two, partly for the pleasure of carrying something warm.
[Narrator] We had built schools and hospitals. We carried beans, coal, timber, and people who could now make journeys their grandparents had scarcely imagined.
[Narrator] I believed in these things. Even now, I remember the particular satisfaction of tracing a consignment through several stations and finding that nothing had been lost.
[Narrator] Above our office stairs hung a proclamation of harmony among the five peoples. Inside, the best rooms belonged to Japanese officials, and Chinese clerks waited outside them with their hats in their hands.
[Narrator] Lihua worked at the table nearest mine, translating claims. She kept a bicycle advertisement inside her ledger and was saving for the model with a lamp.
[Narrator] Her Japanese was excellent. My Chinese was improving under her impatient instruction.
[Lihua] 您又把这两项加了两遍。
[Narrator] I had added two entries twice. She pointed with the blunt end of her pencil, protecting the paper from another of my mistakes.
[Narrator] I gave her a sweet potato. She put it beside the ink bottle to cool and went on checking my arithmetic.
[pause 1]
[Narrator] When boots sounded in the corridor, our junior clerk turned his newspaper face down. I did the same with mine, although I had only been reading the weather.

# Scene 3: The office, before noon
[pause 2.5]
[Narrator] Before noon, Lihua brought me a disputed payment from the goods yard. A loader named Zhang had three days' wages outstanding.
[Narrator] Beside his name, someone had written that he had left his employment. His wife had come twice to ask for the money.
[Lihua] 他不是自己走的。是警察把他带走的。
[Narrator] He had not left of his own accord. The police had taken him away.
[Narrator] Lihua kept her finger on the amount. The nail was stained with ink; beneath it lay the price of several meals.
[Narrator] I asked whether there had been some misunderstanding. She moved her finger away and waited for me to finish reading.
[pause 1.5]
[Narrator] In Mori's room, the stove was red behind its little grille. He drew a chair nearer to it for me.
[Narrator] My superior had helped us find a dentist when Aiko was ill. He remembered the names of his clerks' children.
[Mori] 確認が取れるまで、支払いは保留です。
[Narrator] Payment would remain suspended until confirmation arrived. I said the work had already been done.
[Narrator] Mori looked towards the corridor. On his desk lay a circular bearing an army seal, with our own instructions clipped beneath it.
[Mori] 余計なことは書かないほうがいい。
[Narrator] Better not to write anything unnecessary.
[Narrator] I had intended to record what Lihua had told me. Instead, I asked permission to change the entry to a neutral phrase: payment awaiting clarification.
[Narrator] Mori nodded. I returned to my desk feeling that I had prevented an injustice from becoming permanent.
[Lihua] 那么，他妻子明天还得再来吗？
[Narrator] Would his wife have to come again tomorrow?
[Narrator] I said I hoped to have an answer soon. Lihua folded the bicycle advertisement and put the claim on top of it.

# Scene 4: The railway house, evening
[pause 2.5]
[Narrator] That evening, at home, Aiko was repairing the lining of my coat. Outside, someone was beating a mat against a wall.
[Narrator] I told her about the amended entry. While I spoke, I drew a thread from my cuff and wound it tightly round one finger.
[Aiko] それで、その人はどこにいるの？
[Narrator] Where was the man himself?
[pause 1.5]
[Narrator] I did not know. I had sent the paper to another department.
[Narrator] Aiko bent over the coat again. We ate, discussed a letter from her sister, and decided we could afford the cinema on Sunday.
[Narrator] Later I opened the winter timetable to plan a journey home. Across the page the little figures stood in their reassuring columns, each arrival allowing for a departure somewhere else.
[Narrator] Aiko asked me to hold the coat while she cut the thread. I put the timetable down and lifted the heavy wool between us.
[pause 1.5]
[Narrator] I cannot remember whether Zhang's wife received the money. I remember that coat, its satin lining, and the small pale place where Aiko had pricked her thumb.
[Narrator] By morning, ice had grown over the opening in the window. She breathed on the glass, and I stood behind her, waiting to see what sort of day it was.
